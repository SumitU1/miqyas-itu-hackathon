# Candidate policy and standards gaps (found while building KB v1)

**Status: candidates, not findings.** Each one comes from reading the KB texts and running KB queries on 2026-10-26. None has been checked by a UAE lawyer or a regulator; some depend on documents still missing from the KB (see `KB_COVERAGE.md`). They are inputs for the "policy and standards gaps found" section of the report (day 9-12), where each needs a global example from a public source and a "what would settle it" step.

The brief asks for **domain-specific gaps and global examples, not country-specific recommendations**. The wording below is therefore kept at domain level (consumer health AI), with the UAE text as the evidence. Chunk ids are as of build `20261026T1646Z`.

| # | Candidate gap (domain level) | Evidence in the KB | What would settle it |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Which regime governs health-related data made outside a health service?** The general data-protection law steps aside for "Personal Health Data that has legislation regulating its protection and processing"; the health-ICT law applies to ICT use "in health fields" and restricts offshore handling of data "related to the health services provided in the State". Self-uploaded labs, wearable streams and coach chats held by a consumer app that is not a licensed facility sit between the two texts. | `UAE-PDPL-2021:0004` (Art. 2(2)(e)); `UAE-FL2-2019:0005` (Art. 2); `UAE-FL2-2019:0016` (Art. 13) | Written view from the UAE Data Office and MOHAP; or counsel's opinion. |
| G2 | **Does explicit consent legitimise sending health data abroad?** The data-protection law lets personal data leave the country with the data subject's explicit consent (Art. 23(1)(b)). The health-ICT law bars offshore storage or processing of health data unless a health-authority decision defines the case, and the ministry's summary of the implementing resolution lists specific cases with no consent case. If both apply, consent alone may not be enough. | `UAE-PDPL-2021:0017` and `:0018` (Art. 23); `UAE-FL2-2019:0016`; `MOHAP-MD51-2021:0001`; `DHA-HIS-2024:0028` (Dubai: PHI not to leave the UAE except Decision 51/2021 cases, with DHA approval) | Full Arabic text of Ministerial Resolution 51/2021; ruling on which law prevails for consumer apps. |
| G3 | **Is a consumer wearable a "simple medical device" for the localisation exemption?** The ministry's brief names "information related to simple medical devices and tools" as an exempt case but, in the English summary, gives no definition or threshold; copies must stay in the UAE. | `MOHAP-MD51-2021:0001` | Full resolution text; device classification (`UAE-FDL38-2024` is still missing from the KB). |
| G4 | **Two emirate-level AI-in-health regimes, one federal AI document that is a guiding framework.** Dubai's policy and Abu Dhabi's policy each reach AI developers that use that emirate's population or patient data, and they differ in maturity (Dubai v1, 2021, stated revision date now passed; Abu Dhabi v0.9, 2018). The federal AI Charter calls itself "a guiding framework". A nationwide direct-to-consumer AI coach that is not a licensed facility therefore faces overlapping rules and no single federal rule-owner in the KB. | `DHA-AI-2021:0001`; `DOH-AI-2018:0001`; `UAE-AI-CHARTER-2024:0001` | Check for newer DHA/DoH versions; confirm whether a federal AI-in-health instrument exists. |
| G5 | **Advertising rules do not mention AI-generated or personalised health claims.** The advertising texts in the KB set prior-approval duties and list prohibited expressions ("guaranteed", "no side effects", and others) for conventional adverts. A KB query on AI-generated or automated recommendations in health advertisements returned only weak, off-target evidence (best score 12.2, ITU report). Approval duties are split across three documents. | `NMC-AD-GUIDE:0004`-`:0005` (prior approval); `DHA-MEDAD-2022`; `MOHAP-HEALTHAD-GUIDE`; query run: `python kb/search.py "AI generated content or automated recommendations in health advertisements"` | Current media law and executive regulations (the NMC guide may predate them); consumer-protection law (still missing). |
| G6 | **Breach-notification timing is delegated to regulations the KB does not hold.** The data-protection law requires a controller to notify the regulator and the data subject but sets the period in its Executive Regulations. Whether those regulations have been issued is not confirmed by this project (secondary sources disagree). | `UAE-PDPL-2021:0009`, `:0010` (Art. 9) | Primary text of the Executive Regulations, or a statement that none exist. |
| G7 | **Human review of automated decisions is a right on request, with no stated standard.** The data-protection law lets a person object to an automated decision and requires the controller to "include the human element" in reviewing it on request, but gives no time limit, qualifications or record-keeping standard for that review; the Dubai AI policy says AI is "supportive technology" that does not replace human decision-making and must be "supervised/monitored by end users", and the AI Charter lists human oversight as a principle, but neither sets a review procedure. | `UAE-PDPL-2021:0013` (Art. 18); `DHA-AI-2021:0006`, `:0007`; `UAE-AI-CHARTER-2024:0002` | Executive Regulations; any DHA/DoH guidance on review procedures. |
| G8 | **Standards gap (Y.3172).** Y.3172 gives a "policy" node that applies policies to model output and a sandbox, but no vocabulary for *which* policies (legal rules, consent state, licensing status) a health deployment must evaluate there, or for recording the legal basis of an automated action. This project proposes the Policy & Legal Adviser as that node and logs the cited provision per decision. | `ITU-Y3172` (P node, ML sandbox); `ITU-AIREADY-2` (Dimension 10, AI & Policies) | Review with ITU mentors (sessions on 31 Oct and 7 Nov). |
